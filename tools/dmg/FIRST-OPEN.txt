Fevom, build @BUILD@: opening it the first time
==============================================

This build is signed ad hoc, not by a registered developer, so
macOS refuses its first open until you allow it once. Fevom needs an Apple
silicon Mac with macOS 26 or later.

1. Open Fevom-@BUILD@.dmg and drag Fevom.app onto the Applications folder
   beside it. Then eject the disk image.

2. Allow it, one of two ways:

   a. In Terminal (one command, nothing to click):

        xattr -dr com.apple.quarantine /Applications/Fevom.app

   b. Or in the Finder: open Fevom.app. macOS says it cannot verify it;
      click Done. Then System Settings > Privacy & Security, scroll to
      "Fevom was blocked to protect your Mac", click Open Anyway, and
      confirm with your password. Open Fevom.app again.

3. Fevom opens and offers its setup: fev and docker on your PATH (a
   fenced block in your shell's profile, taken back by "fev setup
   --undo"). Two steps are optional and asked separately:
     - the admin helper (your password once): containers' and machines'
       own IP addresses and *.fev.local names on this Mac, and ports
       below 1024;
     - trusting fev's own root for HTTPS on *.fev.local (one dialog).

4. Open a new terminal: docker, docker compose and fev work, and docker
   commands go to fev (its Docker context is made the current one; the
   one you had before comes back when you remove fev).

What to expect with a build like this one:
  - macOS asks again for what you allowed (Files and Folders access, the
    firewall's question about incoming connections) after each new build
    you install: the permission belongs to the build.
  - Updating: a Fevom whose menu has Fevom > Check for Updates... updates
    itself: it checks once a day, shows what is new and installs when you
    say so, quitting and reopening Fevom. Otherwise, by hand: quit Fevom
    (its menu bar item > Quit), drag the new Fevom.app onto Applications
    and choose Replace, then open it. Either way it stops fev's older
    build and starts its own. Your containers, images, volumes and
    machines stay; running machines start again, and containers do if their
    restart policy says so (as after a restart of Docker). Nothing to type:
    setup is not asked again.

Removing Fevom: choose Uninstall Fevom... in the app's menu, or in Terminal
     fev uninstall
which takes back everything fev set up and removes the app. Your
containers, images, volumes and machines stay unless you also delete them:
     fev uninstall --delete-data
